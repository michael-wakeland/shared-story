1. The Core UI: The "Infinity Timeline"
Instead of a vertical feed where old posts "die" at the bottom, the UI is a horizontal axis.

The Navigation: Users swipe left/right. You can have "Quick Jump" markers for years or major events (e.g., 2025: The Bathroom Remodel or 2026: The Oregon Coast Trip).

Thread Clusters: A "Thread" appears as a vertical stack or a "bubble" on the timeline. Clicking it opens the full conversation, photos, and AI summaries.

Visual Weight: Major milestones (weddings, graduations) appear larger on the timeline, while daily "check-ins" are smaller dots, creating a visual rhythm.

2. "Thread" Permissions as "Invitations to the Story"
In a typical clone, everyone sees everything. Here, you use Granular Participation:

The "Core" Story: A main timeline every family member sees.

Sub-Stories: You create a thread for a specific event (e.g., "Planning the Napa Valley stops"). You invite only the people going.

The Hand-Off: Once that trip is over, you "Publish to Timeline." The private planning chat disappears, and a beautiful, AI-curated summary of the best photos and highlights is "pinned" to the family's shared history.

3. The "AI Curator" (Leveraging your Digital Fridge Logic)
Since you’re already looking at AI for summarizing family media, this is the engine that makes the "Shared Story" low-effort for users:

Contextual Summaries: If a thread gets too long (e.g., 50 messages about where to eat in Taormina), the AI generates a "TL;DR" at the top of that timeline bubble.

Memory Surfacing: On the "Today" marker of the timeline, the AI could show a "Flashback" bubble from the same date 5 years ago, allowing a new thread to start underneath the old memory.

Automated Tagging: It identifies people and locations (e.g., "Salmon Creek Hike") and suggests which "Chapter" of the family story the media belongs to.
